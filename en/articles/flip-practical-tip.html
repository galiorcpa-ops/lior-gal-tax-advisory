A Practical Tip for Handling an Israeli Flip

In short

The green-track tax ruling is meant to simplify an Israeli flip, but the exhausting part is not the Israel Tax Authority – it is the shareholders. The tip: at the very start, give the company one table that collects all the shareholder information, built on the ruling's rights-holder table, so the information is gathered once rather than again and again.

A quick reminder: what is a flip

In a flip, the shareholders of an Israeli company transfer their shares to a foreign company, usually a US company, in exchange for shares in it in the same holding ratios. After the flip, the US company holds all the rights in the Israeli company – with no tax paid at the time of the flip.

The problem: going back to the shareholders again and again

The process depends on the full cooperation of all rights holders, and you reach out to them repeatedly along the way:

  • Before filing: to collect information
  • After filing: to obtain clarifications
  • After the ruling is approved: for signatures

In parallel, the legal process runs as well: signing agreements, depositing shares with a trustee, and more.

The tip: one table for all the information

At the very start of the process, give the company a single table that collects all the information on the shareholders – what the ruling needs, what the forms filed after it need, and what the agreement needs.

Build it on the rights-holder table of the green-track ruling, so it serves as a direct substitute for it, and add internal columns for your own use.

What the table gives you

This table is not just for the Tax Authority – it mainly serves you. It gives you:

  • A quick snapshot of each investor: residency, and rights held beyond shares (SAFEs, warrants, etc.).
  • Early identification of easy-to-miss issues that require disclosure or clarification in the ruling – for example, an investor holding an individual ruling (such as investment funds), cessation of Israeli residency, or immigration to Israel.
  • Filling in Forms 1512 at the end of the process without going back to the investors.

That way the information is collected once, not again and again.
