"Interested Public": A Tax Arrangement You Won't Find in the Ordinance

In short

In a share exchange under Section 104H of the Israeli Income Tax Ordinance, the tax deferral is limited in time (24 and 48 months) and the shares are held by a trustee. In the Israel Tax Authority's practice, an additional arrangement has developed for shareholders from the public, known as the "interested public" (tzibur me'unyan): the tax is deferred until the actual sale, and there is no trustee. The arrangement applies only to "unidentified" shareholders, but sometimes officers who hold a small stake and are not interested parties can be included as well.

Share exchanges and tax deferral under Section 104H

When a public company is acquired in a share exchange, its shareholders receive shares of the acquiring company (sometimes in addition to cash). A share exchange is a "sale" subject to capital gains tax. To defer the tax, Section 104H of the Ordinance can be used, which allows a deferral when the acquirer is a public company (other routes exist, but sometimes 104H is the right one or the only one available).

But the deferral is time-limited: for half of the shares the tax is deferred for 24 months, and for the rest for 48 months. If the shares have not actually been sold by then, a deemed sale takes place. To enforce this, the shares are deposited with a trustee.

The "interested public" arrangement

As an exception to the rule, an additional arrangement has taken root in the Israel Tax Authority's practice for shareholders from the public, known as the "interested public". The term does not appear in the Ordinance, and the arrangement has two main advantages over Section 104H:

  • The tax is deferred until the actual sale – with no 24- or 48-month limit. The assumption is that the public sells its shares within less than two years anyway.
  • No trustee – the banks and brokers holding the shares review the sale and withhold the tax.

Who does it apply to?

The arrangement applies only to "unidentified" shareholders. Controlling shareholders, registered shareholders and interested parties remain under the regular Section 104H arrangement, even when they hold a negligible stake.

The practical tip

Sometimes shareholders who are among the driving forces of the company can also be included in the interested public arrangement, for example officers who hold a small stake and are not interested parties. That is why it is important to know the arrangement, to ask for it, and to know which shareholders to place in it and which in the regular Section 104H arrangement.
