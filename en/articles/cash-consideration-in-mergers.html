Cash Consideration in a Merger – the Cap Isn't Necessarily 49%

In short

Business restructurings are tax-exempt on condition that no cash consideration is received. Mergers have an exception: alongside shares, cash of up to 49% of the total consideration may be received. But 49% is not always the cap – the limit depends on several factors, and sometimes a higher share of cash is possible.

The rule: consideration in shares only

To carry out a merger, or any business restructuring, on a tax-exempt basis under the Israeli Income Tax Ordinance, there is a central rule: no cash consideration may be received in the restructuring, other than shares in the absorbing company or in the company to which the asset was transferred.

The merger exception: up to 49% in cash

An exception to this rule applies to mergers: alongside the consideration in shares, cash consideration of up to 49% of the total consideration in the transaction may also be received. 49% is the number everyone knows, but a close reading shows that sometimes a higher share of cash can be received.

The cap depends on several factors

In fact, the limit depends on several factors, among them the rights holders who remain in the company. To be clear: rights holders who received cash consideration do not enjoy the exemption, but the other rights holders and the merging companies will.
